Key takeaways

  • Corrected age = chronological age ? (40 ? gestational weeks).
  • It adjusts a preterm baby's age to reflect their due date, not their birth date.
  • Typically used until about 24 months.

What is corrected age?

Corrected age (also called adjusted age) is a premature baby's chronological age minus the number of weeks they were born early. Because preterm infants miss part of the development that normally happens in the womb, comparing them to full-term babies of the same chronological age can be misleading. Corrected age gives a fairer picture of developmental milestones.

The corrected age formula

First find weeks early: 40 ? gestational age at birth. Then subtract that many weeks from the chronological age. A baby born at 32 weeks was 8 weeks early, so an infant who is chronologically 6 months old has a corrected age of about 4 months. The calculator above does this automatically once you enter the gestational weeks.

Until what age is corrected age used?

Most pediatricians use corrected age until around 24 months, by which point the gap typically becomes negligible. Your child's care team may advise otherwise based on individual circumstances. For background, the U.S. CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early." program offers milestone guidance.

Need the standard figure instead? Use the main chronological age calculator, or the age in months calculator for screening tools that report months.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate corrected age?

Corrected age equals chronological age minus the weeks born early (40 minus gestational weeks). A baby born at 32 weeks is 8 weeks early, so subtract 8 weeks.

Until what age is corrected age used?

Usually until about 24 months, after which the difference is clinically negligible. Follow your pediatrician's guidance.

Is corrected age the same as gestational age?

No. Gestational age describes how far along the pregnancy was at birth. Corrected age adjusts the baby's current age after birth to account for prematurity.
