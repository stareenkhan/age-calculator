How to Calculate Chronological Age

By the ChronoAge Editorial Team — Updated June 2026

Learning how to calculate chronological age by hand takes just three subtractions. This guide walks through the formula, the borrow-and-regroup method, and worked examples in years and months — the same math our chronological age calculator automates.

Key takeaways

  • The chronological age formula is test date ? date of birth.
  • Subtract day, then month, then year, borrowing when a column goes negative.
  • Report the result as years, months, and days — or as total months for testing.

The chronological age formula

Write both dates as three columns — year, month, day — with the test date on top and the date of birth beneath it. Then subtract each column. The formula is simply:

Chronological age = Test date (Y/M/D) ? Date of birth (Y/M/D)

Calculating by hand, step by step

  1. Set up the columns. Test date over date of birth: Year | Month | Day.
  2. Subtract the days. If the top day is smaller, borrow: take 1 from the month column and add the number of days in the previous month to the day column.
  3. Subtract the months. If the top month is now smaller, borrow: take 1 from the year column and add 12 to the month column.
  4. Subtract the years. What remains is the age in years, months, and days.

Worked example

Test date 2026-06-03, date of birth 2014-11-13:

  • Days: 3 ? 13 is negative, so borrow. May has 31 days, so 3 + 31 = 34; 34 ? 13 = 21 days. Month becomes 5.
  • Months: 5 ? 11 is negative, so borrow a year: 5 + 12 = 17; 17 ? 11 = 6 months. Year becomes 2025.
  • Years: 2025 ? 2014 = 11 years.

Result: 11 years, 6 months, 21 days. You can verify this instantly with the chronological age calculator.

Expressing the result in months

For assessments, convert to months: 11 years — 12 + 6 = 138 months (plus 21 days). Our age in months calculator shows this directly, and the testing calculator formats it as years;months for score sheets.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Dividing total days by 365. Years vary in length, so this drifts off by days.
  • Borrowing a fixed 30 days. Use the actual number of days in the previous month.
  • Using today instead of the test date. For assessments, always use the administration date.
  • Ignoring prematurity. For preterm infants, you may need corrected age.

For occupational and speech therapy contexts, professional bodies such as ASHA describe how age is used in standardized scoring.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for chronological age?

Chronological age = test date ? date of birth, computed column by column in years, months, and days, borrowing where a column is negative.

How do you calculate chronological age by hand?

Write the test date above the date of birth as Y/M/D, then subtract the day, month, and year columns, borrowing from the column to the left when a result is negative.

How do I calculate chronological age in months?

Multiply whole years by 12 and add the extra months. For 11 years 6 months that is 138 months.
